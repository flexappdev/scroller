# AI Enters the Lab

**Snapshot:** 26 September 2026 · Europe/London.

## Why this story matters

This week's strongest AI-science story is not another benchmark. It is a new workflow for discovery.

Anthropic says Claude helped identify a previously uncharacterized enzyme system while searching huge DNA-sequence databases. The company calls the system **array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART)**. The finding emerged from a search involving roughly **950 AI agents over 21 hours**, after which human scientists performed the physical laboratory work. Anthropic says the repeat structure resembles CRISPR arrays, but the biological function of ART remains unknown.

Nature highlighted the result on 25 September, and Reuters reported the announcement on 23 September. The important point is not that an AI has replaced scientists. It is that the division of labor is changing: agents can explore enormous search spaces, generate and critique hypotheses, while humans choose what is worth testing and physical experiments decide what is real.

## What is verified

- Anthropic says Claude identified the ART system while analyzing genomic data.
- The company reports that roughly 950 agents searched for 21 hours using around 210 million tokens.
- Anthropic says the agents gathered more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, generated about 3,500 candidate systems and narrowed them to 20 detailed candidates.
- Human scientists performed all wet-lab experiments.
- ART is associated with repeated DNA sequences reminiscent of CRISPR arrays, but its main function remains unknown.
- Nature and Reuters independently reported on the announcement and Anthropic's new physical biology lab.

## What remains uncertain

- ART's natural biological function.
- Whether the system can become a programmable biotechnology tool.
- How often this AI-agent workflow will produce genuinely novel, reproducible discoveries.
- The true false-positive rate and economic efficiency of large parallel agent searches.
- How well the method generalizes beyond genomic discovery.
