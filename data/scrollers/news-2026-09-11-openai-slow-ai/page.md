# OpenAI Is Talking About Slowing the AI Race

**Snapshot:** 11 September 2026 · Europe/London.

## Why this story matters

Reuters reported on 11 September that Sam Altman told OpenAI staff the company is open to slowing AI development. Two days earlier, OpenAI publicly argued for mandatory capability-based national AI safety requirements and said safety confidence should increasingly set the pace of frontier AI progress, including slowing or stopping development or deployment when a system cannot be sufficiently safeguarded.

The significance is larger than one company meeting. Frontier labs are now explicitly discussing **pacing** as an engineering and governance control: capability evaluations, alignment gates, cybersecurity thresholds, independent assessment, incident reporting, human approval and emergency interruption can determine whether a system proceeds.

## What is verified

- Reuters reports Altman told staff OpenAI is open to slowing AI development.
- OpenAI is publicly calling for mandatory capability-based national AI safety regulation.
- OpenAI says global standards should address when and how frontier development should slow or stop.
- OpenAI says it will slow or stop development or deployment when unacceptable safety risk cannot be sufficiently mitigated.
- OpenAI describes automated AI research, stronger trajectory monitoring and deployment-safety gates as increasingly important as capability grows.

## What remains uncertain

- There is no public universal threshold that all frontier labs agree should trigger a slowdown.
- The Reuters staff-meeting report does not describe a permanent halt or a fixed timetable for slowing development.
- International enforcement, audit authority and cross-lab coordination remain unsettled.
- Future evaluation results can change the risk assessment.
